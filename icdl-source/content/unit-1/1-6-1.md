Ever wonder why the periodic table is shaped like that? Well… yeah because Mendeleev grouped columns through properties, but also because of orbitals!

The periods (horizontal rows) distribute the “principle energy level” of elements, also called an “electron shell.” As the principal number “n” increases, the energy level gets higher. And the probability density extends farther outwards, away from the nucleus.

The principle energy level can then be divided again into sublevels, labelled s, p, d, f, and so on. Do NOT confuse sublevels with orbitals! Sublevels are what contain orbitals, for example, the p sublevel will contain all the p orbitals.

There are also “blocks” on the periodic table, which are also… labelled s, p, d, f, and so on… yes chemistry can get very confusing. Blocks are rectangular sections of the periodic table grouped by which sublevel is being filled. The s-block goes from columns 1 and 2 on the left, as well as helium. The p-block is columns 13-18 on the right, the d-block is columns 3-12 in the middle, and the f-block is those two rows you always see at the bottom of the table, called the lanthanide and actinide series.

For example, if you choose an element from the s-block, you know that the current orbital being filled is an s-orbital. Knowing blocks and sublevels makes it easy for scientists to figure out the orbital configuration of elements, and it can even help you find elements more easily the next time you look at a periodic table.
