I assume that by taking interest in higher level chemistry, you already have a strong understanding in basic chemistry history usually taught in earlier programs, such as the evolution of the atomic models. In this lesson, we will start on the planetary model of the atom, first proposed by Ernest Rutherford and later refined by Niels Bohr.

Do note that Rutherford’s original model was rudimentary and designed on the foundations of classical dynamics and physics, which Bohr later modified by introducing quantum postulates. Although many previous atom models can be described as being planetary, this is simply the term used to describe the classical atoms before the quantum model. We will discuss this later on in this lesson, and explain why this model of the atom is unstable according to classical dynamics.

![Planetary model of the atom: three electrons on circular orbits around a central nucleus.](images/1.3.1-bohr_model.png "This model, although popular as an intuitive image of the atom, failed to fundamentally explain much of the atom’s quantum properties.")

You may be able to recall Rutherford’s gold foil experiment, which proved that atoms are mostly empty space with a positive nucleus, very similar to the planetary model used. For the sake of simplicity, this experiment will not be explained in this lesson, though I highly recommend reading about it if you have not learned about it before.

Understand that, the planetary model was not simply “wrong,” it was remarkably accurate for its time and could predict much of the atom’s behaviours. It was built off the culmination of previous models, as well as many foundational experiments and theories.

I will provide one example of how the classical atom fails to explain atomic stability, and then discuss the quantum atom in a future lesson.

Let’s start with the classical picture people imagine when discussing an atom: an electron, negatively charged, moving in a curved orbit around the positively charged nucleus. If you have taken a physics course in secondary before, then you should immediately realize something is off!

That’s because it is impossible for an object to move in a circular motion without accelerating! Even if its speed is constant, the direction and therefore the velocity of the electron is always changing. Therefore, the electron is accelerating.

What does this mean? Since electrons are negatively charged, electrodynamics states that they must be continuously radiating energy outwards in the form of electromagnetic waves.

Explaining this answer requires an extensive background in complicated electrodynamics, and so will not be developed further in this lesson. Just know that an accelerating charged particle must radiate energy outwards, as the changes in its electric field requires time to propagate through space.

So, if the electron is radiating energy outwards, how does it continue to move in orbit? If we continue following this logic, then electrons should eventually spiral into the nucleus as they lose more and more energy.

![Planetary model with an electron spiralling inward toward the nucleus as its orbit shrinks.](images/1.3.1-shrinking_model.png "Based on the classical model, electrons should radiate their energy outwards, shrinking their orbit more and more until it spirals into the nucleus.")

There are, of course, many other ways that the classical model can be disproven, such as with the results of atomic spectra. Listing all of these counterexamples would be redundant, though I highly recommend reading about them if you are interested.
