What even is the quantum mechanical model? How does the quantum mechanical model solve much of the issues of its predecessors, specifically with atomic stability? To answer, we must look deeper into the “quantum” part of this model.

Up to this point, we have often pictured electrons as particles travelling around the nucleus in an orbit. However, in the quantum mechanical model, electrons exhibit both particle-like and wave-like behaviour. Rather than travelling along a definite path, an electron is described by a wavefunction, which tells us the probability of finding the electron at different locations around the nucleus.

You may recall standing waves from physics, also called fixed waves or stationary waves. These are simply waves where the vibrating pattern stays in a singular position, appearing stationary.

![A standing wave drawn as two overlapping sine waves with fixed nodes along a dotted centre line.](images/1.3.2-standing_wave.png "A diagram of a standing wave, an electron obviously does not actually look like this, but the point is that an electron has similar behaviours to a standing wave.")

These allowed wave patterns correspond to specific energy levels, or quantum states. The electron cannot simply have any energy it wants, only certain states are permitted.

This helps explain atomic stability. In the classical model, an electron orbiting the nucleus would continuously accelerate and therefore continuously release electromagnetic radiation, causing it to lose energy and eventually spiral into the nucleus.

The quantum model does not describe electrons as particles travelling along classical orbits. Instead, an electron can occupy one of these stable quantum states with a specific energy. As long as it remains in this state, it does not lose energy and spiral into the nucleus.

The lowest possible energy state is called the ground state. Because there is no allowed state with a lower energy, an electron in the ground state literally cannot lose any more energy and collapse into the nucleus.

The quantum model is an incredibly difficult concept to understand and grapple over, and so we will discuss its implications more when we talk about orbitals. For now, remember that electrons act like standing waves that can only occupy certain quantum states, the lowest of which having a finite amount of energy and spatial distribution. Since there is no lower state for the electron to fall into, atoms can remain stable.
